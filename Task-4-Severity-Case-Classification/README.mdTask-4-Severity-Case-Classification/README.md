Task 4: Severity & Case Classification

1. Introduction

Severity and case classification are important steps in pharmacovigilance. They help healthcare professionals evaluate the intensity of adverse drug reactions (ADRs), determine their seriousness and identify whether the reactions are expected or unexpected.

2. Objectives

- To understand severity and seriousness of ADRs.
- To classify ADRs as mild, moderate or severe.
- To identify serious and non-serious adverse reactions.
- To differentiate expected and unexpected ADRs.
- To evaluate the clinical impact of ADRs.

3. Classification of ADRs

A. Based on Severity

1. Mild: Symptoms are easily tolerated and require minimal or no treatment.
2. Moderate: Symptoms cause discomfort and may interfere with daily activities or require medical treatment.
3. Severe: Symptoms are intense and may significantly affect normal functioning or require substantial medical intervention.

B. Based on Seriousness

Serious ADR: An adverse reaction resulting in death, life-threatening conditions, hospitalization, prolonged hospitalization, significant disability, congenital anomaly or another medically important event.

Non-serious ADR: An adverse reaction that does not meet any regulatory seriousness criteria.

C. Based on Expectedness

1. Expected ADR: A reaction already mentioned in the applicable approved product information.
2. Unexpected ADR: A reaction not mentioned, or inconsistent in nature or severity with the applicable approved product information.

4. ADR Case Studies

Note: The following are hypothetical educational cases. Expectedness classifications assume the stated reaction is or is not listed in the applicable reference safety information.

Case 1: Mild Nausea

- Suspected drug: Amoxicillin
- Adverse reaction: Mild nausea after medication administration.
- Severity: Mild
- Seriousness: Non-serious
- Expectedness: Expected (assumed listed in product information).
- Clinical impact: Minimal discomfort without significant interference with daily activities.
- Final classification: Mild, non-serious and expected ADR.

Case 2: Severe Headache

- Suspected drug: A hypothetical medication.
- Adverse reaction: Severe headache affecting the patient's daily activities.
- Severity: Severe
- Seriousness: Non-serious (no hospitalization, life-threatening condition or other seriousness criterion).
- Expectedness: Expected (assumed listed in product information).
- Clinical impact: Significant discomfort and reduced ability to perform normal activities.
- Final classification: Severe, non-serious and expected ADR.

Case 3: Anaphylactic Reaction

- Suspected drug: Penicillin.
- Adverse reaction: Severe allergic reaction with difficulty breathing and a sudden fall in blood pressure.
- Severity: Severe
- Seriousness: Serious (life-threatening condition requiring emergency medical intervention).
- Expectedness: Unexpected (assumed not listed in the applicable reference safety information).
- Clinical impact: Immediate medical emergency requiring prompt treatment and monitoring.
- Final classification: Severe, serious and unexpected ADR.

Case 4: Gastrointestinal Bleeding

- Suspected drug: Aspirin.
- Adverse reaction: Gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalization.
- Severity: Severe
- Seriousness: Serious (hospitalization).
- Expectedness: Expected (assumed listed in the applicable product information).
- Clinical impact: Significant blood loss requiring medical intervention and hospital monitoring.
- Final classification: Severe, serious and expected ADR.

6. Importance in Pharmacovigilance

- Helps identify clinically important adverse drug reactions.
- Supports appropriate medical intervention.
- Helps identify unexpected safety signals.
- Supports accurate ADR documentation and reporting.
- Contributes to patient safety and risk management.

7. Conclusion

Severity, seriousness and expectedness are essential elements of ADR evaluation. Correct classification helps pharmacovigilance professionals assess clinical impact, prioritize serious cases and improve medication safety.
